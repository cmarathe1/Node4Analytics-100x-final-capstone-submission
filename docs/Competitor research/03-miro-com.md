# Reference #3 — miro.com

**Date analyzed:** 2026-04-09
**URL:** https://miro.com/
**Relevance:** The dominant infinite-canvas collaboration platform on the market. The most direct product-metaphor reference for FinQuira — Miro IS an infinite canvas used by professionals, and its landing page must solve the exact same structural problem: communicating a complex canvas-based interaction model to a first-time visitor. High resources, mature brand, recent visual-identity refresh. The critical reference for Q5 (canvas visualization), Q10 (canvas tool landing pages), and Q14 (interactive demo scope).

---

## What Miro Is

Miro is a collaborative visual workspace built around an infinite, multiplayer canvas. Originally a digital whiteboard for distributed teams, it has expanded into a broader "innovation workspace" with an AI layer ("Intelligent Canvas"), autonomous agents ("AI sidekicks" like Product Leader, Agile Coach), 6,000+ templates, and 250+ integrations. The current hero is positioned around the 2026 Canvas event and the Intelligent Canvas narrative. Audience is broad — 100M+ users, 250,000+ companies, from individuals to the Forbes-500 — which forces their marketing to operate at a broader, more emotional register than a niche vertical tool.

Miro is not audience-identical to FinQuira. Miro targets cross-functional creative teams (product managers, designers, researchers, strategists); FinQuira targets investment analysts. Miro is post-IPO-scale and can lean on customer logos; FinQuira is pre-launch. The comparison is **structural and metaphorical** — how does a canvas product explain its canvas to a first-time visitor, and what compositional decisions does every canvas tool seem to share?

## Visual Identity Analysis

### Color Palette

Miro completed a full visual identity refresh (the "Miro VIS" system, developed with AKQA). The refreshed palette is built around:

| Role | Value | Notes |
|------|-------|-------|
| Hero spot color | **Miro Hero Yellow** — `#FFD02F` (Sunglow) | The single load-bearing brand color, used sparingly, "always in a manner that highlights the most crucial aspects of our narrative" |
| Primary surface | **Cream** / Alabaster — `#FAFAFA`–`#FFFFFF` | Warm off-white primary ground, not cold grey-white |
| Deep brand ink | **Stratos** — `#050038` | Near-black with blue undertone, used for type and depth |
| Secondary accent | **Dodger Blue** — `#4262FF` | Saturated cobalt, used for links and interactive elements |
| Tertiary accent | **Leapfrog Lime** (bright green) | Freshness accent in illustrations and feature moments |
| Tertiary accent | **Next Level Lilac** (soft purple) | Depth and texture accent in illustrations |

**Assessment:** Miro's palette is emphatically light-ground, warm-cream, and anchored by a single saturated hero color (yellow). The philosophy of "yellow sparingly, always highlighting the crucial moment" is identical in principle to FinQuira's "single desaturated accent used only for primary actions and critical attention points" — but executed at the opposite temperature and saturation. Miro's yellow is joyful, inviting, high-saturation. FinQuira's copper is institutional, restrained, low-saturation. Same structural discipline, opposite emotional register.

The tertiary lime and lilac accents only appear inside illustrations and characters, never as page-level structural color. The page itself is essentially a **three-color system**: cream ground, Stratos ink, yellow hero accent — with blue for interactive elements. This is the most important palette lesson for FinQuira: **even a brand with a full rainbow of accents restricts the landing-page surface to three load-bearing colors.** Restraint is structural, not a FinQuira-only idea.

**What this confirms for FinQuira:** The single-hero-accent architecture is the canvas-product pattern. Miro restricts its landing-page palette almost as tightly as FinQuira's hypothesis demands. The lesson is not "add more colors" — it is "commit harder to three colors and make them load-bearing."

**Why the light ground is wrong for FinQuira anyway:** Miro targets broad approachability — design teams, workshop facilitators, educators, cross-functional collaborators. Cream + yellow reads as "creative, playful, welcoming." That emotional register is correct for their audience and lethal for FinQuira's. An equity analyst reading a cream-and-yellow landing page will close the tab in under three seconds. The warm-dark charcoal ground remains the correct inverse for institutional finance.

### Typography

Miro's typography is the weakest specific-identity signal on the page. Their brand uses a contemporary grotesque sans-serif for all display and body (reporting is inconsistent across sources, but the rendered page reads as a restrained Neo-Grotesk in the Söhne / GT America family, set at moderate tracking). There is **no serif anywhere on the Miro landing page.** No display serif, no editorial pull-quote, no accent face.

Hero headline is set large but not enormous — visually dominant within the fold but not pushing into the magazine-headline scale that Notion commits to. Tracking is moderate. Weights are confined to regular and medium, with occasional bold.

**Assessment:** Miro's typographic identity is carried by **scale, pairing with illustration, and brand color**, not by the typeface itself. This is the third canvas/collaboration tool in a row (after n8n and Notion) that defaults to a geometric or grotesque sans with no serif presence. **The serif-display gap in this category is now very well-established.** Three of the most visible canvas/workspace products on the market all default to sans. Nobody in this space is using a display serif. FinQuira's serif-display hypothesis keeps getting stronger with every reference we analyze.

The deeper lesson from Miro: when your brand has strong illustration language, custom characters, and a load-bearing hero color, the typeface is allowed to recede and do structural work only. FinQuira does **not** have that luxury — it has no illustration language, no custom character set, no product screenshots (pre-launch), and a monochromatic warm-dark ground. That means FinQuira's typography has to do more identity work than Miro's does, not less. The serif is not decorative — it is load-bearing in a way Miro's sans is not.

### Surface Treatment

- Flat, borderless card surfaces (same principle as Notion — cards differentiated by color fill, not by border or shadow)
- Generous rounded corners on most container elements — feels approachable, not industrial
- Illustration characters appear layered over flat color fields, often overflowing their containers slightly to create informal energy
- Mild drop-shadows on some elevated elements, never heavy
- **No glassmorphism, no atmospheric gradient decoration, no backdrop-blur effects** — Miro has entirely left the "dark SaaS gradient glow" aesthetic behind

This matches both n8n's restraint against decoration and Notion's flatness principle. **Three references in a row have abandoned glassmorphism.** FinQuira's rejection of it is well-supported.

### Illustration Language

Miro's rebrand leans heavily on a custom illustration system: characters with "bold, clear outlines, unusual perspectives, and sharp features," built from a 3D model workflow to enable personality at scale. The characters appear throughout the landing page as the primary visual texture — not photography, not screenshots as the lead visual.

**Assessment:** Illustration-as-primary-visual is a valid strategy for a broad creative product but the wrong move for FinQuira. Analysts will read custom characters as "consumer product," not "professional instrument." FinQuira's visual language must come from the product's own metaphor — nodes, connections, flows — rendered as abstract diagram elements, not as humanized characters. **Miro confirms by contrast that the correct FinQuira visual language is node-diagram, not character-illustration.**

## Layout & Composition

### Grid System

Miro uses a standard 12-column grid with generous side padding on desktop, stepping down through breakpoints. Max content width appears to sit around 1280–1440px, with the hero breathing wider. Section padding is substantial — content is never edge-to-edge on desktop.

### Hero Structure

The hero is **left-anchored asymmetric** — headline and subhead occupy the left column, a large visual element (video or animated product fragment) occupies the right. This is the same compositional pattern Notion uses and the same one FinQuira has already committed to. **Three canvas/collaboration tools in a row now use left-anchored asymmetric heroes** (Notion, Miro, with n8n as the centered-symmetric counter-example). The asymmetric left-anchored pattern is becoming an established category signal.

Above the hero itself, there is a thin event-promotion bar ("Canvas 26 — Save your spot") — a timed promotional strip that sits above the true hero without competing with it. This is an interesting discipline: promotional content is structurally separated from the hero rather than cluttering it. FinQuira doesn't need this right now but the principle is useful — **above-hero promotional strips are a clean way to handle time-sensitive content without compromising hero discipline.**

Hero composition elements:
1. Left column — headline "Get from brainstorm to breakthrough with Miro," subhead, two CTAs ("Sign up free" / "Watch video")
2. Right column — large animated or video visualization of the canvas in action
3. Everything else deferred below

The hero does one thing: asserts one idea, shows one visual, offers one primary action. Feature navigation is handled in the top nav bar, not in the fold.

### Section Rhythm (Top to Bottom)

1. **Above-hero promotional strip** — Canvas 26 event announcement
2. **Primary navigation** — Product / Solutions / Resources / Pricing + account CTAs
3. **Hero** — headline + subhead + CTAs + canvas visualization
4. **Social proof bar** — "100M+ people collaborating on Miro" + "250,000 companies"
5. **Flow from idea to outcome** — template/format showcase section ("Choose a starting template") showing templates like Retrospective Summary, Product Brief, Research Synthesis, Meeting Notes
6. **Metrics / customer proof** — hard numbers (3.6x faster time to market, 50% shorter planning process, 2x faster time to market) with a headline like "The only thing more important than moving fast..."
7. **Feature tile grid** — six capability cards (AI, Intelligent Canvas, Formats, Blueprints, Enterprise Security, Integrations) — grid-based, not strict bento
8. **Solution highlight** — Product Acceleration (deeper dive into one use case)
9. **Customer testimonial** — Lucy Starling / ASOS quote with narrative framing
10. **Getting-started CTAs** — secondary entry points (templates, integrations, learn more)
11. **Comprehensive footer** — deep navigation, solutions by industry, resources, company

### Information Density

Miro's density is **moderate**. Each section has room to breathe, large type carries the headline work, and no single section overloads the viewport. It is denser than Notion (more sections, more information per scroll) but sparser than a financial publication or Bloomberg terminal.

**Assessment for FinQuira:** Miro's density is **still too sparse for the analyst audience**, but closer to the right calibration than Notion's. Miro sits at the midpoint — generous whitespace, but more content per viewport than Notion's extreme breathing room. FinQuira should sit between Miro and a financial publication — denser than Miro, structurally more rigorous. This gives us a useful density triangle:

```
Notion (sparse editorial)  —  Miro (moderate)  —  FinQuira target  —  Bloomberg/FT (dense editorial)
```

FinQuira's target sits between Miro and Bloomberg — denser than Miro, more editorially composed than Bloomberg. Miro validates that the middle of this range is a real, inhabited design space.

### Editorial Ambition

Miro's landing page is **less editorially ambitious than Notion's**. It reaches for energy and warmth (illustration characters, yellow accent, conversational copy) rather than editorial gravity. Section headlines are outcome-oriented and conversational ("Flow from idea to outcome in seconds") rather than magazine-chapter declarative. The hero is confident but not enormous-scale in the Notion sense.

This is a deliberate positioning choice: Miro wants to feel **approachable and collaborative**, not **editorial and authoritative**. The right choice for a broad creative audience. The wrong choice for FinQuira's analyst audience, who need the editorial authority signal. **Miro confirms that editorial-serious is live design territory that Miro has explicitly chosen not to occupy — leaving room for FinQuira to own it in the analyst segment.**

### Feature Composition

Miro uses a **six-tile feature grid** — six capability cards (AI, Intelligent Canvas, Formats, Blueprints, Enterprise Security, Integrations). The grid is more uniform than Notion's asymmetric bento; cards are closer to equal-span than Notion's varied widths. Each card carries its own visual (illustration, UI fragment, or icon), short headline, brief description, and CTA.

**Assessment:** Miro's more uniform grid is safer and less editorially ambitious than Notion's bento, but still avoids the monotonous 3-column-icon pattern of generic SaaS. It suggests a continuum:

```
Generic SaaS 3-column icon grid  —  Miro 6-tile moderate grid  —  Notion asymmetric bento  —  Full editorial spread
```

FinQuira should sit at Notion's level or slightly beyond — asymmetric spans, varied card sizes, rhythmic rather than uniform. Miro is the "safe moderate" version; FinQuira needs to commit further.

## Canvas Metaphor Communication — Most Detailed Section

This is the critical question for FinQuira and the main reason Miro is a high-value reference. How does the dominant infinite canvas platform explain its canvas to a first-time visitor?

### What Miro Actually Shows in the Hero

The hero visualization is **not a live interactive canvas embed**. It is a curated, animated, or video-based fragment that shows the canvas *in use* — cursors moving, sticky notes appearing, connection lines forming, templates loading. The visual is designed to be *watched*, not *interacted with*. Visitors see the canvas being used by an implied team, not the canvas waiting for them to use it.

This is a deliberate choice: a cold-start live canvas is a terrible first-visitor experience. An empty grid with a "click anywhere to start" prompt is intimidating, unclear, and forces the visitor to do work to understand the product. A curated video or animation shows the end state of the canvas in a way that communicates "this is what it looks like when it's working" without requiring the visitor to figure it out.

**Lesson for FinQuira:** Never show an empty canvas. The hero visualization must be the canvas in its **assembled, connected, working** state — nodes populated, connections drawn, workflow resolved. The visitor should see the end-state of an analyst's workflow, not a blank template waiting to be filled. The node-connection animation FinQuira has already committed to is compatible with this principle — connection lines draw themselves into an already-populated workflow, not into an empty canvas.

### What Miro Does Not Do

Miro does **not** embed a live interactive Miro board on the homepage. There is no "try it in your browser" playground at the top of the fold. The nearest thing to an interactive demo is the template selection interface further down the page ("Press '/' for options or start writing") which lets visitors preview template archetypes — but this is a template chooser, not a full canvas experience.

This is a striking absence given that Miro has every technical capability to embed a real canvas. They have chosen not to, and the reason is almost certainly conversion discipline: **a live canvas in the hero competes with the headline and dilutes the conversion path.** Visitors who start playing with a canvas stop reading the copy, lose the narrative, and fail to reach the CTA. Miro has tested this. The answer is: show the canvas, don't give it to them in the fold.

**Lesson for FinQuira (Q14):** A single interactive element is worth including, but **not in the hero fold**. The interactive moment belongs mid-page, after the visitor has already absorbed the hero narrative and is in an exploratory mindset. Notion's cost calculator and Miro's template chooser both live mid-page, not in the fold. FinQuira's interactive element (whatever form it takes — node assembly vignette, before/after slider, workflow micro-demo) should live in section 3 or 4, not section 1.

### How Miro Uses Canvas Visual Language as Page Design

This is where Miro is genuinely clever and where the strongest adoptable lesson lives. The page doesn't just *show* canvas content — it **uses canvas visual language as the design system of the page itself**. Specific techniques:

1. **Sticky-note-style cards** — feature cards on the page echo the shape, corner radius, and color blocking of actual Miro sticky notes. The page's own card system *is* the product's card system, flattened to 2D.
2. **Cursor presence** — implied multi-user cursors sometimes appear in section visualizations, carrying the "multiplayer" signal into static page elements.
3. **Connection-line decoration** — some section transitions use thin curved lines that look like the connector arrows inside a Miro board. The page's own connective tissue is rendered in the canvas's own visual idiom.
4. **Template-card previews** — rendered as if they were cards sitting on a canvas, with slight rotation and layering that mimics the informal placement of objects on a real board.

**This is the single most important adoptable principle from Miro.** The canvas metaphor is communicated not through screenshots of the canvas but through **making the landing page itself read as an abstracted canvas**. The page is the product's visual grammar.

**Lesson for FinQuira:** This principle is directly portable and already aligned with existing direction. The landing page should use node-and-connection visual language as its **structural design system**, not just as a decorative element in the hero. Specifically:

- Section transitions can use thin connector lines that evoke node-graph edges
- Feature cards can be rendered as node-shaped containers (rectangular with small connection ports on their edges)
- The layout itself can read as a canvas — elements visually linked by implied lines, bento cells as nodes, structural alignment as the grid of a workflow diagram
- The hero's connection-line animation is not an isolated flourish — it is the opening statement of a design language that continues throughout the page

Miro's implementation of this is softer and more decorative than FinQuira's should be (rounded sticky-note shapes, soft curves), but the **architectural principle is the same**: let the product's own visual language be the design system of its marketing page. FinQuira's version should be more rigorous, more grid-like, more engineering-document than whiteboard-sketch — but the structural move is identical.

### The Canvas Tool Design Consensus

Across n8n, Miro, and (by inference) other canvas tools, a few decisions seem universal:

1. **Left-anchored asymmetric hero** with headline on the left and visualization on the right (n8n is the outlier with a centered layout — and n8n is the weakest landing page of the three)
2. **No live canvas embed in the hero** — always curated animation, video, or static composition
3. **Flat card surfaces, no glassmorphism** — all three abandoned atmospheric decoration
4. **Single load-bearing accent color** — yellow (Miro), orange (n8n), soft blue (Notion)
5. **Scroll-revealed feature capabilities** — not crammed into the fold
6. **Sans-serif typography with moderate scale** — nobody in the category uses a display serif

The consensus confirms that items 1–5 are category norms worth adopting. Item 6 is the category gap FinQuira exploits. This is a sharp strategic picture: **conform on the structural decisions, differentiate on the typographic and tonal decisions.** FinQuira should look like a canvas product in layout and composition, but feel like a financial publication in type and tone.

## Motion & Interaction

### Page Motion

Miro's page motion is **restrained and functional**. There is no aggressive scroll-jacking, no parallax, no continuous animation. The hero visualization contains the most motion on the page — curated animation or video — and after the hero, motion is limited to fade-in reveals on viewport entry, hover states, and occasional micro-interactions inside feature cards.

The hero visualization uses motion to communicate the canvas metaphor: cursors moving, sticky notes appearing, connections forming. This motion is **load-bearing** — it's carrying the concept, not decorating the page. It is also **contained** — it lives inside the hero visualization only, not leaking into the surrounding layout.

### Signature Moment

Miro does have something closer to a signature motion moment than n8n did: the hero canvas visualization is bespoke, concept-carrying, and unique to Miro. It is not stock-fade animation. Whether it rises to the level of a true signature depends on execution — but Miro has at least invested in a differentiated hero motion.

**Assessment for FinQuira:** Miro validates the architectural shape of the signature motion hypothesis. The hero visualization carries the concept through motion, lives in a contained area, and does not spill into the rest of the page. FinQuira's node-connection-draw animation should follow the same principle: contained to the hero visualization slot, concept-carrying, bespoke, and not leaking into the rest of the page. Everything outside the hero visualization should be the quiet fade-on-viewport-entry pattern.

### Scroll Behavior

Standard scroll with restrained fade-in reveals on viewport entry. No parallax. No pinned sections. No scroll-jacking. This matches FinQuira's existing direction.

### Hover and Interaction

Cards show moderate hover states — slight elevation, color shift, or secondary content reveal. Nothing aggressive. Touch devices substitute tap-to-reveal on elements that rely on hover to expose secondary information.

## Copy Strategy

### Hero

- **Headline:** "Get from brainstorm to breakthrough with Miro"
- **Subhead:** "Collaborative AI workflows that help your team build the right thing, faster"
- **CTAs:** "Sign up free" / "Watch video"

The headline is **outcome-oriented but generic**. "Brainstorm to breakthrough" is a safe, evocative phrase that describes the creative process arc. It works for Miro's broad audience because any creative team self-identifies with the brainstorm-to-breakthrough arc. It would be actively wrong for FinQuira — analysts do not think of their work as "brainstorming" or "breakthroughs."

The subhead is more interesting: "Collaborative AI workflows that help your team build the right thing, faster." This introduces three specific concepts — collaborative, AI, workflows — and anchors them with the "build the right thing" outcome. The phrase "the right thing" is a subtle product-market-fit nod that resonates with product teams.

**Assessment:** The hero copy works harder than n8n's method-first copy and is less bold than Notion's "Meet the night shift." It sits in the middle — outcome-oriented but not personified, specific enough to be credible but broad enough for the audience. This is the conservative-confident middle of the copy spectrum.

**Lesson for FinQuira:** Miro's middle-of-the-road copy confirms that the editorial-bold end (Notion) and the method-first end (n8n) are the two extremes, with the safe middle being Miro's territory. FinQuira's hero copy should lean toward the **Notion-editorial end** — one crisp outcome-first proposition, high commitment, low hedging. Analysts respect commitment; they are allergic to safe middle-ground copy that sounds like a committee wrote it.

### Section Headlines

- "Flow from idea to outcome in seconds"
- "The only thing more important than moving fast..." (metrics section)
- "Intelligent Canvas — Empower teamwork on one, infinite, multiplayer canvas"

These are **conversational and outcome-focused**, softer than Notion's chapter-title headlines but tighter than n8n's benefit-language headlines. They carry the "collaborative and fast" positioning consistently.

### Tone

- Confident but warm — "breakthrough," "flow," "empower," "build the right thing"
- Emotionally engaged, not clinically neutral
- Speaks to team outcomes, not individual capabilities
- Avoids hyperbolic verbs (no "supercharge" / "revolutionize")

**Assessment for FinQuira:** Miro's warmth is the wrong tone for the analyst audience. The analyst audience wants **clarity, specificity, and authority** — not warmth, not collaboration language, not outcome-hype. FinQuira's copy register should be closer to Notion's chapter-title editorial voice, but tuned for the institutional-finance reader — more declarative, more specific, more restrained. Think "Your research workflow, one canvas" rather than "Get from insight to investment thesis."

### Handling Broad Multi-Use Product

Miro solves the "product does many things" problem through **solution-based navigation** (Product Acceleration, Business Acceleration, industry verticals like Financial Services) and **template-based feature showcasing** (retrospectives, product briefs, research synthesis, meeting notes). The page doesn't try to describe every feature — it gives visitors entry points by role, industry, and template.

**Lesson for FinQuira:** FinQuira does not have the same breadth problem. It is a vertical tool for one audience (analysts) doing one job (research-to-report workflow). FinQuira should **not** adopt Miro's solution-based navigation or template-gallery approach — those exist because Miro's product surface is too broad to describe linearly. FinQuira's narrower scope is an advantage: the landing page can tell one coherent story from hero to CTA without needing a "choose your own path" navigation layer.

### Social Proof

- "100M+ people collaborating on Miro"
- "250,000 companies"
- "6,000+ templates" / "250+ apps and integrations"
- Customer quote (Lucy Starling, ASOS)
- Forbes-class logos implied throughout

This is heavy scaled-product proof. Not portable to pre-launch FinQuira. The pre-launch social proof strategy remains specificity-of-problem-description and domain credibility, as previously established.

## Key Insights for FinQuira

### Adopt

1. **Use the product's own visual language as the page's design system.** This is the single most important Miro lesson. The landing page should read as an abstracted canvas — section transitions using connector lines, feature cards rendered as node-shaped containers, the layout itself echoing workflow topology. This is already in FinQuira's direction but Miro validates and extends it: the principle should apply across the entire page, not just in the hero visualization.

2. **Single load-bearing accent color, used only at critical attention points.** Miro's "yellow sparingly, always at the crucial moment" is architecturally identical to FinQuira's single-copper-accent discipline. Same principle, opposite temperature.

3. **Left-anchored asymmetric hero with contained concept-carrying motion.** Now validated by both Miro and Notion. Three canvas/workspace tools in a row confirm this as the category norm. n8n's centered symmetric hero is the category outlier and the weakest of the three. FinQuira's committed asymmetric hero direction is well-supported.

4. **Never show an empty canvas.** The hero visualization should show the canvas in its assembled, connected, working end-state. Nodes populated, connections drawn, workflow resolved. The visitor should see what it looks like when it's working, not a blank template waiting to be filled. This applies specifically to FinQuira's node-connection animation — it should draw into an already-populated workflow, not from scratch.

5. **Interactive demo element belongs mid-page, not in the fold.** Miro and Notion both place their interactive elements (template chooser, cost calculator) mid-page after the visitor has absorbed the hero narrative. FinQuira's Q14 answer: whatever interactive element we build, it lives in section 3 or 4, not in the hero. The hero stays disciplined.

6. **Moderate-to-restrained motion, signature moment contained to hero visualization only.** Miro's approach is architecturally identical to FinQuira's direction: one concept-carrying hero moment, quiet fade-on-viewport-entry everywhere else.

7. **Above-hero promotional strip as a structural pattern.** If FinQuira ever needs to promote a time-sensitive event or launch beat, a thin strip above the hero (not competing with it) is the clean solution. Not needed now but worth keeping in the kit.

### Reject

1. **Light cream ground with saturated yellow accent.** Correct for Miro's collaborative creative audience, lethal for FinQuira's analyst audience. Warm dark charcoal + desaturated copper remains the correct inverse.

2. **Character illustration as primary visual texture.** Miro's custom 3D illustration characters work for their broad creative audience and would actively destroy credibility with analysts. FinQuira's visual texture comes from node-graph abstraction, not humanized characters.

3. **Conversational warm tone.** "Brainstorm to breakthrough," "flow from idea to outcome," "build the right thing" — these phrases are warm and energetic and wrong for the analyst register. FinQuira's copy should be declarative, specific, and restrained.

4. **Rounded sticky-note-like card geometry.** Miro's cards echo their product's rounded sticky notes. FinQuira's cards should echo their product's structural node-graph geometry — rectangular, grid-aligned, precise. No soft informal corners; subtle radius at most.

5. **Solution-based navigation and template gallery.** These exist to manage Miro's broad product surface. FinQuira's narrower scope is an advantage — one coherent linear narrative is stronger than a "choose your path" navigation layer.

6. **Six-tile uniform feature grid.** Miro's moderate grid is safer than Notion's asymmetric bento. FinQuira should commit further — asymmetric spans, varied card sizes, rhythmic rather than uniform. Notion's bento is the right reference; Miro's is the conservative fallback.

7. **Moderate density.** Miro is closer to right than Notion but still too sparse. FinQuira sits between Miro and a financial publication.

### Differentiation Opportunity

Miro is the third canvas/workspace product in a row (after n8n and Notion) to use sans-serif-only typography, moderate information density, and a non-editorial tonal register. The category is converging on a consistent set of design moves. **The serif-display + dense-editorial + authoritative-tonal combination is unoccupied space in the canvas-tool landing page category.** FinQuira can own it decisively.

Miro also confirms by contrast that the **editorially serious / financially authoritative** positioning is empty territory in this space. Miro has explicitly chosen warmth and approachability. Notion has chosen confident-editorial-but-sparse. n8n has chosen technical-maker. Nobody in the category is occupying "institutional finance editorial." That position is FinQuira's for the taking.
