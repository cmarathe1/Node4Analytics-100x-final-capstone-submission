### Comparability: two false incompatibilities, surfaced by rung 8 (2026-09-04, ADR 0100)

**Owner: rung 6a-i's comparator. NOT fixed in rung 8** — changing it moves `incompatible` counts,
which expires every downstream baseline ([`LESSONS.md`](LESSONS.md) §11), and it is belief-layer work
inside a Track-A slice. Rung 8 made it VISIBLE by rendering the blocker string an analyst can read,
which is the correct relationship between a surface and the layer beneath it.

Both are live on `ws-infosys-foundation` today:

1. **A scale difference is reported as a definition conflict.** `cash_conversion_earnings_quality`
   is `incompatible` at Q3 FY2025 and Q1 FY2026 because the frames differ by
   `{'unit_family': 'usd mn'}` vs `{'usd bn'}` (and `mn` vs `bn`). Those are the same unit at
   different scales and convert exactly. Calling them incomparable is a **false negative** that
   suppresses a real comparison — the opposite failure from ADR 0086's manufactured conflicts, and
   just as costly, because the analyst is told a comparison cannot be made when it can.
2. **`not_applicable` is compared as if it were a reporting basis.** `funding_liquidity/FY2026`
   blocks on *"different reporting basis (not_applicable vs consolidated)"*. `not_applicable` is the
   ABSENCE of a stated basis, not a rival one. Comparing our own missing metadata against a real
   basis and calling the result a conflict is ADR 0086 exactly: **a finding must show what IS there,
   never what our machinery manufactured.**

The fix likely belongs with the frame comparator: normalise scale within a unit family before
comparing, and treat an unstated facet as *unknown* (which blocks nothing on its own) rather than as
a distinct value. Both need a must-not-fire set — `usd mn` vs `inr mn` must STAY incomparable, and
so must two genuinely different bases.

### 2a. Deferred *out of* 1E by ADR 0049 / D43 (2026-07-19) — groundwork landed, feature parked

The 1E design review proposed a much larger slice. These four were cut with their **groundwork
persisted** (the D36 pattern: park the feature, land the field), so picking them up later needs no
rework of what 1E built. Each is right *eventually* — none has a producer or a test bed today.

- **Disclosure episodes / research events.** One earnings episode = results filing + fact sheet +
  presentation + call transcript + subsequent broker updates. Treating those as independent
  confirmations **inflates source independence**; treating them as one artifact loses structure. Also
  real in the corpus: some Infosys transcript PDFs contain a **media conference AND an analyst call**,
  and AI Day is a strategy event, not a results call. **1E lands a nullable `episode_id` on the
  document passport** (user-assignable, heuristic-defaulted) so Phase-2 independence counting has the
  field; the lifecycle/UI is deferred. *Blocked on corpus:* no complete earnings bundle exists in D13
  yet (§8·3 requests the pieces).
- **NSE XBRL structured filings outranking PDF extraction.** NSE exposes structured XBRL for financial
  results, RPTs, BRSR, credit ratings and more — including bank/NBFC-specific forms. For structured
  facts these should **outrank** parsing a PDF of the same content. Track D connector work feeding
  **Lane 2**; pairs naturally with "Lane-2 numeric-KPI extraction" above. *Note:* the Companies Act
  §129 itself exempts banking/insurance from the universal corporate statement format — one more
  reason business-model packs beat a single "financials" schema.
- **The expectations plane** (consensus, estimate revision, surprise, guidance-vs-actual variance,
  market-implied baseline). This is what separates *foundational-research-ready* from
  *decision-ready* — you cannot test "what was already priced in" without price, estimates, consensus
  or a user model. **Phase 3+, and explicitly NOT faked before then**: ADR 0049's readiness levels
  exist so the product says plainly that company documents reach the second level and not the third.
- **A PROV-O-grade evidence plane as a separate build.** Deliberately *not* deferred so much as
  **already largely built** — 1C (immutable mentions, runs, content-addressed snapshots, receipts),
  1D (typed elements, section classes, transcript turns, span-level citations) and D28 (resolved
  issuer identity + `::publisher` voice) cover most of it. What remains is completing it inside 1E,
  not standing up a parallel system. Keep the W3C PROV distinctions (entity/agent/activity,
  derivation, quotation, primary source, revision) as a **review checklist** for citation semantics:
  a citation says *where*, not *what kind* — quote vs. deterministic calculation vs. company
  assertion vs. broker estimate vs. AI interpretation.

### Found by the rung-6d sign-off (ADR 0116–0119) — deferred, none blocking

### Found by the 2026-08-30 rungs-6b/6c/6d sign-off (ADR 0088–0093)

- **Ask cannot read the period vocabulary its OWN citations print** — the one gap this slice
  measured and deliberately did not close. Lane-2 citations name the column label verbatim, so an
  analyst reads `Year ended March 31, 2024` and asks it back; **9 of 63** such labels are period
  words `match_period` can parse, and the router's period cue only knows `FY25` / `Q3` shorthand.
  Widening the router ALONE would be worse than the status quo: a question naming March 2024 would
  reach the numeric lane, `match_period` would return `None`, and the analyst would be served the
  **latest** period under a question naming another — a plausible number on the wrong period, this
  lane's named failure mode. **The router and `match_period` must move together**, with the safe
  Indian-FY case first (a March year-end date is unambiguously that fiscal year; Jun/Sep/Dec dates
  need a span the question does not state). `citation_vocabulary_gap` in `lane2_probe` fails the day
  either half moves alone. Sized as its own slice, not a regex tweak.
- **A bare metric question does not reach the numeric lane** — *"HDFC Bank advances"* routes to
  semantic, because the router needs a period or a numeric cue. `answer_kpi` explicitly supports
  "no period → latest available, named explicitly", so the capability exists and is unreachable
  through Ask. Deliberately NOT changed here: the same cue requirement is what correctly keeps
  *"how has HDFC grown its advances over three years?"* on the narrative route, so loosening it is a
  routing-policy decision (ADR 0017/0018) with blast radius across every question in the product.

### Found by the 2026-08-27 rung-6a-ii sign-off (ADR 0087)

- **Tests that retrieve CORPUS-WIDE are coupled to whatever the shared dev store contains.** A
  fixture document's chunks are embedded with `HashEmbeddingProvider` while the real corpus uses the
  production model, so an unscoped query fills its 20 candidates from a different embedding space
  and the threshold drops all of them — 0 hits against 3 when scoped. One test broke this way and
  was fixed by scoping it to its own document (the REAL path, invariant #6). *Worth a sweep:* any
  other test that asks unscoped against the dev store has the same bomb, and it detonates silently
  as the corpus grows.
- **`fact_versions` accumulates `ws-test-*` debris the way `fin_facts` did** — 7 rows after a suite
  run, one per test workspace. Harmless, and *not* cleaned automatically, so a future reader must
  not read a non-zero count as real facts. The `fact_probe --store` line reports the number plainly
  for that reason. Revisit if it ever obscures a real baseline.
